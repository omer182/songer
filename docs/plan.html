Songer

A Songless-style "name that tune" game that uses your own Spotify library as the song source. Play it solo, battle friends online with a room code, or split the family into teams around the TV. Self-hosted on the homeserver next to geoquest.

What Spotify allows in 2026

Spotify tightened its developer platform a lot in 2024–2026. These rules shape the whole design, so here they are before anything else.

The four constraints

  • 5 Spotify accounts max. A personal ("Development Mode") app can only be used by 5 Spotify accounts that you add by email in the dashboard. Public access needs a registered business with 250k monthly users. So only you (and up to 4 others) can sign in with Spotify. Everyone else plays as a guest.
  • You need Premium. The app owner's account must have Premium, or the app stops working.
  • No more 30-second previews from Spotify. preview_url is gone. Spotify audio only plays through the Web Playback SDK, which needs a Premium login on that device and doesn't run in phone browsers.
  • The developer policy prohibits games and trivia quizzes. For a private app on your server with a few family accounts, the practical risk is Spotify revoking your Client ID. This app could never go public on Spotify's API. I designed it so Spotify is one replaceable input. It only answers "which songs do I like?"; it doesn't supply the audio clips for online play.

How the design handles it: Spotify is the library (what you like, your top tracks, your playlists, a band's albums). The audio comes from Deezer's free public 30-second previews, matched song by song. Deezer previews need no login and play on any phone. That means guests never need Spotify. On a laptop or TV where you're signed in, the app can use the Spotify player to play the real song intro.

Connection

How it connects to your Spotify

Standard "Sign in with Spotify" (OAuth Authorization Code flow). The secret and your refresh token stay on the homeserver. The browser only gets a session cookie.

sequenceDiagram
    participant You as You (browser)
    participant S as Songer server
    participant SP as Spotify
    participant DZ as Deezer (public API)
    You->>S: Tap "Connect Spotify"
    S->>SP: Redirect to Spotify login (scopes below)
    SP-->>You: "Allow Songer to read your library?"
    You->>SP: Allow
    SP->>S: Callback with code
    S->>SP: Exchange code for access + refresh token
    S->>S: Store refresh token (encrypted, SQLite)
    You->>S: "Quiz me on Radiohead"
    S->>SP: Artist albums, then album tracks
    S->>DZ: Find a 30s preview for each track
    S->>S: Cache matches, build a 10-song pool
    S-->>You: Round 1 clip (trimmed server-side)

Scopes requested

  • user-top-read: your top tracks and artists (4 weeks / 6 months / all time)
  • user-library-read: Liked Songs and saved albums
  • playlist-read-private: your playlists
  • user-follow-read: artists you follow (fills the "pick a band" list)
  • user-read-recently-played: "what I listened to this week" mode
  • streaming, user-modify-playback-state: optional, only for the real-intro player on the TV

Game modes

Three ways to play

Solo

Quiz me

Pick a source (top tracks, a band, an album, a playlist). Ten songs, six tries each, clip grows with every miss. Also a daily song from your own top 50 with a streak counter.

Online battle

Room vs friends

Geoquest-style rooms. The host picks the source and shares a 4-letter code. Everyone hears the same clip at the same moment. Faster and earlier guesses score more. Guests need no account.

Party · teams

Family night

The TV or laptop is the stage and plays the music. Phones scan a QR code and become team buzzers. First team to buzz answers out loud and the host taps right or wrong. Pick a genre, a decade, or your playlist.

Mockups · solo

Pick a source, then guess

The app is dark everywhere so it looks right on a TV at night. The clip bar is the main visual: pink is what you've heard, and each segment is a longer clip unlocked by a miss.

Mockups · online battle

Room code, same clip, race

Built on geoquest's room system (Socket.IO, room codes, ready-up). New parts: the server tells every client "start clip at server time T", so everyone hears it together, and you can see who has already got it.

Mockups · party mode

TV is the stage, phones are buzzers

Built for a family gathering: one speaker, one screen, everyone in the same room. Nobody types. The host (you, on the laptop) controls the game and judges answers. Teams can mix ages, so it works when grandma only knows the 70s and the kids only know TikTok.

Other people

Who needs what

Short answer: only you need Spotify. Friends and family just open a link.

WhoSpotify account?What they can do
You (host)Yes, PremiumEvery mode, using your library. The real-intro player works on your laptop/TV.
Up to 4 "linked" friendsYes, any planSame as you: solo on their own library, host their own rooms. You add their Spotify email in the dashboard (5-account cap). Unlocks a "Whose song is it?" battle that mixes everyone's top tracks.
GuestsNoJoin rooms and parties with a code and a nickname, play on Deezer clips. Optional: pick a guest name once so their scores keep.

Fairness rule: in online battles everyone hears the same Deezer clip, including you. Mixing "Spotify intro for me, Deezer chorus for you" would give you an advantage.

Scoring

Points

ModeRule
SoloSolved on try 1–6 earns 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 points. A daily streak counts consecutive days solved.
Battlebase × try factor + speed bonus: base 1000, try factor 1.0 / .8 / .6 / .45 / .3 / .2, speed bonus up to 200 shrinking over the round. Wrong guesses cost nothing except time; the clip grows on a shared clock.
PartySong +3, artist +1. A wrong buzz locks that team out for the song and the others may steal. The host can override any call.

Answer matching ignores "Remastered 2011", "- Live", "feat. X", punctuation and case, and accepts small typos. Autocomplete suggests from the round's pool plus decoys from the same artists, so the list never gives away the answer.

Architecture

How it runs on the homeserver

Same stack as geoquest (React + Vite + Tailwind, Node + Express + Socket.IO), plus SQLite for tokens and the track cache. One container serves both the API and the built frontend, on the existing proxy network behind Nginx Proxy Manager.

Details that matter

  • No cheating via dev tools. The server never sends the title before the reveal. Clips come from opaque URLs like /clip/r8f2/3 and are trimmed server-side, so the browser only ever holds the seconds you've unlocked.
  • Synced start. Clients measure their offset from the server clock (ping) and preload the clip; the server says "play at T+1.5s". Everyone hears it within ~50 ms.
  • Deezer matching. Search by title + artist, check that the duration is within 3s, cache the result. Tracks with no match get dropped from the pool, and the pool builder over-fetches to compensate.
  • Spotify redirect URI must be HTTPS: https://songer.omersher.com/api/auth/callback. For local dev, Spotify accepts http://127.0.0.1:5100/… (not localhost).
  • Secrets live in a stack.env kept out of git (your stack review flagged this): SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID, SPOTIFY_CLIENT_SECRET, TOKEN_ENC_KEY, SESSION_SECRET.
  • CI follows geoquest: GitHub Actions builds ghcr.io/omer182/songer; Portainer pulls it. Pin the tag this time instead of :latest.

Decisions before I start

Are you OK with the Spotify policy risk?

Personal use, max 5 accounts, never public. Worst case Spotify disables the Client ID and you fall back to Deezer packs and playlists. If not, I'd swap the library source for Last.fm (if you scrobble) or Deezer-only.

Clip start: intro or "somewhere in the song"?

Deezer previews are a 30s slice, usually from the middle or chorus. True Songless plays the intro, which needs the Spotify SDK and so only works on a Premium-logged-in laptop or TV. My suggestion: Deezer by default, intro mode as a solo/TV option.

Name and subdomain

Working name "Songer" (you already have an empty Projects/songer folder) at songer.omersher.com.

Language

English only to start, or Hebrew + RTL from day one for the family nights?
